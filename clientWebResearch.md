## Part 1: Research

1. [15 Discovery Call Questions Every Website Designer Should Ask](https://localcreative.co/blog/questions-every-website-designer-should-ask-new-clients/) 
This article explains that a discovery call should help biht the client and the designer decide if the project is a good fit. Iy also shows that strong questions should focus on goals, audiance, budget, timeline, and what success looks like for the website.

2. [How to Create Brand Guidelines Your Whole Team Can Follow](https://www.constantcontact.com/blog/brand-guidelines/)
This article explains why a client intake form is useful before a project starts because it collects the business background, goals, features, timeline, and pain points. It showed me that good intake questions help prevent misunderstandings and make the project easier to plan.

3. [How to Create Brand Guidelines Your Whole Team Can Follow](https://www.constantcontact.com/blog/brand-guidelines/)
This page explains that brand guidelines are the "rulebook" for a business's logo, colors, fonts, and voice. It helped me see that a website should match the client's visual identity, or a brand guide should be created if the business is starting from scratch.

4. [Web Development Client Intake Questionnaire Form](https://www.jotform.com/form-templates/web-development-client-intake-questionnaire-form)
This template shows the kinds of imformation a web developer should gather before starting to build, including goals, budget, timeline, website type, and inspiration examples. It reinforced that a good client intake form gets basic details early so the project can be scoped clearly.

## Part 2: Client Questions

### The Business

1. What does your business do, and what makes it different from other businesses in your area?
2. Who owns or manages the business, and what is their background or experience?
3. What are your short-term and long-term goals for the business over the next year?

### The Audience

1. Who is your main target customer, and what are they like?
2. What should a visitor do first when they arrive on your website?
3. How do you want customers to contact you after they visit the site?

### The Content 

1. What pages or sections do you think your website needs?
2. Who will provide the text, photos, and other content for the site?
3. Are there any important facts, policies, or customer stories that must be included.

### Branding

1. Do you already have a logo, business colors, or a style guide for your brand?
2. What three words would you want customers to use to describe your business?
3. Are there colors, logos, or brands you love or want to avoid for inspiration?

### The Look

1. What style do you want your website to feel like: modern, friendly, luxury, playful, or something else?
2. Can you share ant websites you like or dislike. and what do you like or dislike about them?
3. Do you want the site to feel more personal, more professional, or more sales-focused?

### The Project

1. WHat is your deadline fo launching the website?
2. Who will update the website after it is built, and how often will changes be needed?
3. Where will the websute be published, and who will be responsible for keeping it running?
4. Do you have a budget range for the project, and are there any must-have features?
